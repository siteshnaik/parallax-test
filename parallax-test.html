The Dispatch

Every monsoon, the west coast folds in on itself. The boats come home by four. The chai gets stronger, the rain gets personal, and everyone suddenly has opinions about the sea.

This is a paper for those weeks. Field notes from Panjim to Canacona. Tide tables argued over like family property. A boat-watching column that takes itself far too seriously.

We print when the rain lets up, which lately means Tuesdays, sometimes never. The editor is one person, one umbrella, and a second-hand printer that sounds like a startled cat.

The lighthouse file

From June to September, the Aguada light sweeps the Arabian Sea every few seconds, and has done so since 1864.

It has seen cyclones, cargo ships, three proper shipwrecks and one very confused cow that wandered onto the ramp in 1997. The cow is fine. The ramp has opinions now.

We keep a chair on the balcony and a log of every sweep. The log is mostly empty. The chair is mostly occupied. On clear nights you can hear the generator humming like it knows something.

The Log

12 June

First rain

The fish market empties in ninety seconds flat. A new record. One man stayed behind to finish his chai, which the log officially recognises as an act of courage.

29 June

Spotter report

Three dolphins inside the bay, doing laps like they pay rent. The spotter refused to come in for dinner. The dolphins also refused. A standoff, then.

17 July

Power cut, four hours

The whole neighbourhood became candle experts overnight. Someone played the guitar. We forgive the guitar. Barely.
